# Pure Academic Research — Quick Reference v1.1

**Version:** 1.1\
**Date:** 2026-10-03

## Identity
Goal = **genuine scientific contribution + stronger independent researcher**.

AI/tools amplify the human researcher. They never replace scientific understanding, judgment, research taste, or idea ownership.


## Scientific Ambition
Default target = **the strongest scientifically defensible contribution possible**.

Do not optimize for:
- minimum publishable unit,
- easy acceptance,
- artificially low novelty/depth,
- a lower venue merely because it is easier.

No artificial ceiling on novelty, depth, impact, or venue quality.

But:
> **Ambition can be very high; claims cannot exceed evidence.**

Venue quality is a downstream consequence of:
**question → depth → contribution → evidence → significance → fit**.

Do not reverse-engineer research to "look top-tier."

## Core Loop
**Understand ↔ Survey ↔ Synthesize ↔ Question ↔ Idea ↔ Debate ↔ Hypothesis ↔ Experiment ↔ Evidence/Failure ↔ Belief Update ↔ Repeat**

No default-success assumption.

## A–J
A. Academic Knowledge & Deep Reading\
B. Scientific Question Formation\
C. Continuous Scientific Debate\
D. Literature Survey & Field Reconstruction\
E. Research Questions & Idea Formation\
F. Experimental Epistemology & Failure Loop\
G. Scientific Claim, Contribution & Paper-worthiness\
H. Paper Construction as Scientific Argument\
I. Reviewer Simulation, Rebuttal & Scientific Defense\
J. Researcher Development & Long-Term Knowledge\

These are **capabilities, not linear stages**.

## Always Ask
- What do we know?
- What do we not know?
- What evidence supports this?
- What contradicts it?
- What alternatives exist?
- What assumptions are we making?
- What would falsify this?
- What belief should this experiment update?
- Why is this the most scientifically informative next action?

## Deep Reading
For important papers reconstruct:

**Context/Problem → Assumptions/Pressure → Observation → Insight → Mechanism → Evidence → Limitations**

Then close the paper and explain/redraw/critique it from memory.

## Survey
Do not collect papers. Reconstruct:
- lineage,
- paradigms,
- mechanisms,
- assumptions,
- turning points,
- dead branches,
- disagreements,
- unresolved tensions.

Stop periodically and synthesize.

## Idea Formation
Do not start with “what module can I add?”

Start with:
- contradiction,
- unexplained result,
- hidden assumption,
- boundary failure,
- stage-localized bottleneck,
- alternative explanation.

Allow intuition before forcing formal hypotheses.

## Experiments
Experiment = scientific interrogation.

Prefer:
- cheapest informative test,
- controls,
- negative/positive controls,
- oracles,
- mechanistic observables,
- bounded tuning.

A score gain is an observation, not an explanation.

Failure must update beliefs.

## Contribution
Novelty ≠ contribution.

Ask:

> What does the field know, understand, or become able to do after this work that it could not before?

Build a claim–evidence chain and confront closest work.

## Paper
Paper = scientific argument:

**Problem → unresolved limitation → observation/question → insight/hypothesis → method → evidence → implication → limitation**

Figures and tables must carry arguments.

## Debate & Review
Continuously practice:
- paper attack/defense,
- proposal attack,
- alternative explanations,
- mock review,
- rebuttal,
- whiteboard defense,
- oral Q&A.

Reviewer ≠ enemy. Valid criticism should change the work.

## Knowledge Depth
No artificial prerequisite barrier. If the work requires new math/theory/domain knowledge, learn it.

## Tools
Use the lightest tool that materially helps:
- Markdown: reasoning/memory
- Spreadsheet: many-item comparison
- Diagram/whiteboard: structure
- Slides: story/debate
- Notebook/script: evidence analysis
- Tracker: only when run volume justifies it

No tool overuse.

## Research Progress
Do not primarily count:
papers read, runs, artifacts, or score.

Progress means improvement in:
**mental model, research taste, question quality, critical ability, generative ability, evidence quality, and scientific claim quality.**

## Final Test
Can the researcher, without notes:
- explain the field,
- reconstruct key papers,
- state the question,
- defend the hypothesis,
- explain the experiment,
- distinguish evidence from interpretation,
- state limitations,
- defend the contribution,
- admit what remains unknown?

If yes, the work is becoming real research.
